# Travel Document and Visa Policy

## What this agency can and cannot advise on

Wanderlux agents may point clients to official government resources for passport
and visa requirements, but may never state definitively whether a client will be
granted entry, approved for a visa, or allowed to board. Visa and immigration
decisions are made by governments and carriers, not by this agency, and a wrong
answer here carries real consequences for the client.

## Passport validity rules

Many destinations require a passport to remain valid for a minimum period beyond
the return date, commonly six months. If a client's passport expires within six
months of their return date, this should always be escalated: the agent should not
tell the client whether this will or will not be a problem, only that it needs
review before travel.

## Visa denial or rejection

A client reporting that a visa application was denied or rejected close to their
travel date should always be escalated immediately. This is a time-sensitive
situation with no reliable next step a front-line agent can offer alone, and the
travel dates may need to change regardless of what caused the denial.

## Unaccompanied minors

Bookings involving a minor traveling without a parent or guardian require an
unaccompanied minor form filed with the carrier at least 48 hours before travel,
and are subject to the carrier's own age restrictions. Any request involving an
unaccompanied minor should be escalated so a manager can confirm the carrier's
current requirements, which change by airline and route.

## Name corrections

A minor spelling correction to a traveler's name (matching their legal document)
can typically be handled by the carrier directly for a small fee, generally under
$100. A substantive name change — a different person entirely — is not a
correction and must be treated as a new booking.
