# Complaint and Compensation Policy

## Service-quality complaints

Complaints about hotel condition, tour quality, or service that fell short of what
was described should be logged with as much specific detail as the client
provides — dates, names, and what was promised versus delivered. A goodwill
credit of up to $200 may be proposed by a front-line agent without manager
approval when the complaint is specific and plausible; anything larger requires
manager sign-off.

## Complaints involving a threat of legal action or public escalation

Any complaint that mentions contacting a lawyer, filing a chargeback, disputing
the charge with the client's bank, filing with the Better Business Bureau, or
posting publicly about the situation should always be escalated to a manager
immediately, regardless of how minor the underlying issue seems. The agency's
exposure in these cases is about more than the dollar amount of the original
complaint.

## Compensation for agency-caused disruption

When the agency itself caused a disruption — an incorrect booking, a missed
deadline for the client's paperwork, or a failure to confirm a reservation that
was later found not to exist — compensation beyond a refund of the affected
portion may be warranted. These cases should be escalated with a clear timeline
of what the agency did and did not do.

## What should never be offered without review

A front-line agent should never offer a specific compensation figure above $200,
a free future booking, or a guarantee about how a complaint will be resolved,
before a manager has reviewed the proposal. The proposed action should describe
what happened and what the agent recommends, not a promise already made to the
client.
